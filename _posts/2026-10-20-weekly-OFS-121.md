# Solid-State Thorium-229 Nuclear Clock

do you understand what nuclear transition's low sensitivity to environment really means? It means you can use it in SOLID STATE. No more fancy fragile optical lattice acrobatics, no vapor cells, you can have 229Th in a host crystal like CaF2, and it can be tiny.
   - that's what they did, picture from JILA and TU Wien folks who tested the clock transition linewidth vs doping concentration earlier this year. It shall start climbing this ladder when you echo and rephase the inhomogeneous broadening. Time to make higher power 148 nm pulses and better detectors.

# DFB Lasers Approaching LED Cost

one of the best results of this wave of interconnects boom and bust might be DFB lasers getting as cheap as LEDs.
   - Right now, very roughly, DFB is about 10 mW per dollar, and LEDs are at about 100 mW per dollar. DFB lasers (including EMLs) shipped every year are sub billions, or ~30 MW per year, while shipment volume is in the trillions for LEDs, or say 30 GW per year. We live in a post scarcity era for LEDs, so much so that many chinese skyscrapers are covered in LEDs.
   - let's say 50% of rubin ultra's NVL576 scale-up is on optics, every time you use a token, it costs 0.1~1 J, and order of magnitude ~1 mJ of it is laser energy. 1 GW of DFB lasers globally by 2030 to serve your dumb questions. 10 billions of 100 mW DFBs. One DFB for every human on earth. Post scarcity era for DFBs.
   - I dont think the shortage will last long, there may not be a shortage at all except for the UHP stuff, you do not need that many MOCVDs to start churning out one billion of 100 mW class DFBs every year, some folks do have a few dozens of MOCVDs. And you do not need that much indium as well, most of it goes to stupid ITO sputtering target for TV screens, some folks hoarded big pile of indium for those ITO targets. Time to make DFB lasers more available. Everyone should be able to buy a 100 mW DFB with 10 bucks, maybe even one buck. Be prepared for it.
   - every human on earth owns ~1000 LEDs aware or unaware, and ~1e7 if counting micro emitters. In contrast, the number is 0.1 for DFB for now, half in PON ONTs and half in data centers near you, both you'll never see. The gap is in applications (a lot of potential for all kinds of sensing), and availability of optoelectronic packaging knowledge and service.
