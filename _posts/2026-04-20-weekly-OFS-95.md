# Energy Bottlenecks in Quantum Frequency Conversion

energy will very quickly become the bottleneck for a lot of quantum applications once the fidelities and rates are good. Take frequency conversion as an example, between microwave and telecom frequencies, the numbers vary wildly from ~1 pJ/qubit to ~1 uJ/qubit (sometimes people don't try at all and you might get ~1 J/qubit). And you can quickly calculate what qubit rate can you do given a 20 uW cooling power from a typical dilfridge.

For optical quantum frequency conversion (e.g. between visible and telecom), the numbers vary wildly from 0.2 nJ to 4 uJ per qubit, kind of surprisingly good, and not that different from energy per gate. In short, networking atomic/color center qubits will be much easier than networking superconducting qubits.
![20260414_062440_0.jpg](/assets/images/2026/20240502_20260420/20260414_062440_0.jpg)
   - Hu2026: [Efficient bidirectional quantum frequency conversion between telecom and visible bands using adaptively phase-matched III-V nanophotonic waveguides]( https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71688-2)
   - question on where semiconductor quantum dots fall: they support so many different kinds of qubits, I'd guess similar to the above two kinds depending on their frequencies. It has to be converted to telecom photons for non trivial distance of networking.
