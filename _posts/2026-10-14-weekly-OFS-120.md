# Tilted output waveguide facets on transmitter chips

Quiz: why are the output waveguides all tilted at an angle on these transmitter chips? (notice they are not on the receiver chip in the last pic)
![20250127_005007_2.jpg](/assets/images/2026/20240302_20261011/20250127_005007_2.jpg)
![20250127_005007_3.jpg](/assets/images/2026/20240302_20261011/20250127_005007_3.jpg)
![20250127_005007_4.jpg](/assets/images/2026/20240302_20261011/20250127_005007_4.jpg)
![20250127_005007_5.jpg](/assets/images/2026/20240302_20261011/20250127_005007_5.jpg)
   - Answer: mainly to reduce backreflection by terminating the waveguide at a small angle, similar to [angle polished fiber connectors](https://www.rpmclasers.com/blog/why-should-single-mode-fibers-have-an-angle-polished/).
   - However it is not the full story. All these drawings are off because the light would refract and no longer going out horizontally. Hence to correct for that, the waveguide is rotated so that the beam going out is horizontal.
   - Here is a 2D toy model simulation (with an AR layer otherwise it would be way messier), and you can see the reflected beam bouncing off, and the far field of the beam shooting out is refracted upward. Some of the reflected beam still gets out and interferes with the main beam and mess it up, hence better handle your reflected stray light properly.

# Packaging heterogeneous integration on substrate level

i still think many packaging people are not imaginative enough.
