# Spot Size Converter Papers and Cognitive Effort

simple component papers, esp. generic ones where theres zero new idea or surprise, have become more and more infuriating for me. Because of photonics is all analog, theres so much more stuff you can tweak and talk about that makes your component seem new, you can swap one or two materials, you can do it at a bit different wavelengths, different temperatures, or even different fibers, you can swap the resist and boom the sidewall is better and propagation loss is better blah blah. As a result, you learn how to do it once, and you can pump out papers without even using your consciousness.


It does a few bad things. It lowers down the expectation on congnitive efforts of the whole field. You can publish results and get "recognition" without coming up anything new, you get used to making mundane decisions, and you do not even need to make that many decisions to get the seemly new component working, and you expect this is how little congnitively demanding you need to work, and you develop similar expectation for your peers. Meanwhile, the form of exchange stagnant, because any attempt on improve the communication efficiency of this line of work would trivialize much of the efforts, and invalidate the point of writing up a paper. Why are you making four individual plots of parameter sweeps and gather them into a pdf, instead of an interactive webUI that let the audience select whatever they would like to see and check? You could probably distill a category of component papers into a claude skill and claude could do the design part end to end within an hour (including writing up and validating a 2d mode solver).

So your value is now the execution, which you are not even writing any details about. "The chip was then diced and polished to expose the taper input facet." Now thats some decisions and parameters not so trivial, but nope, you are not getting any of that.


( not picking on this spot size converter paper in particular, there's probably hundreds of similar SSC papers every freaking year )
