# Shape Sensing Endoscope Fiber with Bragg Gratings

Shape sensing endoscope fiber: I thought it is sensing shape of the target at the end of the fiber, but it is actually sensing the shape of the fiber itself.
   - It's made with a few twisted fibers with bragg grating, which let's you measure local strains and thus curvatures.
![20241022_041114_0.jpg](/assets/images/2026/20240923_20260904/20241022_041114_0.jpg)

   - Fig. 1-2: Lamb2024: [Shape sensing endoscope fiber](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPTICA.532250)
   - Fig. 3-4: Paloschi2020: [3D Shape Sensing With Multicore Optical Fibers: Transformation Matrices Versus Frenet-Serret Equations for Real-Time Application](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSEN.2020.3032480)
   - The spectrum and the wavelength shift of the bragg grating looks pretty clean. They could measure curvature as small as 2.6 mm, and relative error of the shape with ~ 1%. For large curvature it gets less accurate because of smaller strain. [Bronnikov2019](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.380816)

# Remote Speech and Heartbeat Extraction via Laser Speckle

Or on any part of your body like neck, face chest etc., from ~ 100 m away, thru windows, and "immune" to background noise.
   - It uses interference of laser speckle patterns, and all you need is a 532 nm laser and a ~> 8 kfps camera.
   - Up the price you could do it invisible in IR.

# Distributed Raman Amplification for Ultra-Wideband

distributed Raman amplifications are so nice, no need for specialized fibers, no need for repeaters/OEO conversions, no additional delays. Just a mux for you to launch the pump, which is a shorter wavelength laser shifted by the Raman phonon freq, and you can even pump more wavelengths to get broader gain bw.
   - however, it is weak in fibers, and you need tens of km of fibers for decent gain. So to make it small and relevant in shorter reach scenarios, you start looking for stronger Raman with tighter confinement, and suddenly all the other nonlinearities start to bite you, two-photon absorptions, four wave mixing.. and you start yearning for diamond based PIC because it has such good bulk properties, but most of the diamond PIC demos are for quantum stuff (translation: low power and low bandwidth).
   - photonics folks are still stuck with no single do-almost-everything material like Si and SiO2 did for cmos (at least initially), and instead have to play with a dozen different materials most of which have near zero volume. One for the lasers & amplifiers, one for the modulators, one for the detectors, and one for low-loss routings. Especially amplifiers, such a primitive component, and you still cannot ask for a few nice things at the same time.
